## LinkedIn Post Copy

I almost blew a 50k deal to save eighteen bucks.

Here's the play-by-play.

I had an enterprise pitch across town. Big client. Bigger stakes.

Opened the rideshare app.

Economy: 24, 22 minutes.
Comfort: 42, 18 minutes.

My brain went straight to "why pay more for basically the same ride."

Booked the 24 one. Didn't think twice.

Should've thought twice.

Driver took 7 minutes just to show up.

Then we hit construction. Then side streets. Then more construction.

20 minutes turned into 48.

I'm in the back seat, watching the clock, sweating through my shirt.

Not stressed-sweating. Actually sweating.

Doing the math on how bad this was about to look.

Walked into their conference room with 30 seconds to spare.

Out of breath. Brain still catching up. Trying to act like a normal person.

They were gracious. Didn't say a word about it.

We closed the deal.

But let's be real. That wasn't good business. That was luck.

If they'd had a 2 PM hard stop, I don't lose 18.

I lose a client I'd probably have worked with for years.

Over the cost of a sandwich.

And here's the thing that actually bothers me.

I see founders make this trade constantly. Including past me, apparently.

Cheaping out on the tool that would've saved 10 hours a week.

Waiting one more quarter to make the hire that's obviously overdue.

Going with the vendor who's 200 cheaper and always late.

Every time, it feels like the smart, disciplined choice.

Every time, the math is actually terrible.

Because you're not risking 18. Or 200. Or 2,000.

You're risking whatever sits behind it.

A client. A launch date. A reputation you spent years building.

Small savings, uncapped downside. That's not frugality. That's gambling with extra steps.

The rule I run everything through now:

If speed, punctuality, or trust are on the line, price is not the variable to optimize.

What's the smallest corner you cut that almost cost you the most?
